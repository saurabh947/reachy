# SO-101 follower wrist roll (servo 5): mechanical and assembly causes of stall/overload, and builder fixes

Scope: SO-101 / SO-ARM100/101 with STS3215/ST3215-class servos. Web research only (no hardware touched). Research date 2026-10-13. User's case: Waveshare SO-ARM101 CAM Kit (SE), resin parts F1-F11 (follower) / L1-L12 (leader); follower wrist roll measures ~154° by hand with torque off, leader ~255°; follower servo 5 trips overload (flashing LED) almost immediately in LeRobot teleop.

Part-name mapping used below (inferred from Waveshare step text vs TheRobotStudio file names, not confirmed by any source): Waveshare F8 "wrist bracket" = TheRobotStudio `Wrist_Roll_Pitch_SO101.stl` (holds servo 5, bolts to servo 4's horn); Waveshare F9 "follower wrist" = `Wrist_Roll_Follower_SO101.stl` (the "gripper housing" that holds servo 6 and is bolted to servo 5's horn); leader L9 = `Wrist_Roll_SO101.stl`.

## Q1. What is the normal wrist roll range on an SO-101 follower? Is it limited by the gripper servo cable? Is the leader's range larger by design?

### Takeaway
A correctly built SO-101 wrist roll is NOT continuous: it has a designed printed hard stop and travels about 338°-345° stop-to-stop on both follower and leader (four arms measured in Sep-Oct 2026). The leader is not larger by design. The user's ~154° follower range (and even the ~255° leader range) is far below that, so something extra is blocking the follower. Because the STS3215/ST3215 has no internal mechanical limit, a range that short with torque off must come from printed parts, debris, screws or the servo 5 to servo 6 cable, not from the servo or the horn spline position.

### Cited Findings
- **Measured range, follower (PR #4583, opened 2026-09-08, still open):** One SO-101 follower's wrist roll is blocked in both directions at raw 113 to 3981, which is 3868 counts or about 340°. A plain `lerobot-calibrate` sweep on the same arm recorded 70 to 3946 (3876 counts, 340.7°). The author describes the stop as "a corner of the printed gripper housing meeting the wrist bracket", not a gear stop, i.e. "two printed parts touching". — [huggingface/lerobot PR #4583](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/4583)
- **Measured range, leader vs follower (same PR, second commit and review, Sep 25 to Oct 3, 2026):** The author measured the leader's wrist_roll at 3923 counts (~345°) and the follower's at 3885 counts. Reviewer stepankandrushin measured two more arms: follower 3902 counts (342.9°), leader 3846 counts (338.0°). Summary in review: "four arms with a stop, between 338° and 345°, and none that spins". In one pair the leader range was larger and in the other the follower was, so neither arm is larger by design. — [PR #4583](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/4583)
- **Designed calibration stop on SO-101 wrist parts (TheRobotStudio issue #170, Jul 23 to Aug 15, 2026):** A user thought the wrist lacked a "protrusion to stop wrist rotation". A contributor (dmosher42) replied that the photo was of the SO-100 part, and that the SO-101 `Wrist_Roll_SO101.stl` has the calibration stop and the follower wrist `Wrist_Roll_Follower_SO101.stl` also has one. The reporter replied "Thanks! I was mistaken" and closed the issue. The images could not be checked in page text. — [TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100 issue #170](https://github.com/TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100/issues/170)
- **Other builders confirm hard stops:** In LeRobot issue #3193, user ayerone (2026-06-05) said their SO-101 has physical wrist roll stops and rotates "a little less than 360". User twdent (2026-08-23) said their SO-101 Pro Kit has hard stops. — [huggingface/lerobot issue #3193](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/3193)
- **The servo itself has no end stop:** Waveshare's ST3215 spec lists rotation angle "360° (0~4095)" and "Mechanism limited angle: No Limit". Stall torque is 30 kg.cm @12V (19.5 kg.cm @7.4V) and locked-rotor current is 2.7 A (2.5 A for the 7.4V version). — [Waveshare ST3215 product page](https://www.waveshare.com/product/robotics/motors-servos/servos/st3215-servo.htm)
- **The servo 5 to servo 6 cable crosses the roll joint (Waveshare guide):** In step 14, servo 4's wire enters F8's bottom port, and "A connection cable is also inserted into the other port of the servo 5" and threaded "out of the bottom of the wrist bracket F8". Step 17 says to "Use the connection cable of servo No. 5 to connect with servo No. 6". Servo 6 sits in F9, which is bolted to servo 5's horn (step 16), so this cable runs from the fixed F8 to the rotating F9. — [Waveshare SO-ARM100/101 Kit Assembly wiki](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/SO-ARM100/101_Kit_Aassembly)
- **Builders explicitly call for cable slack at J5:** An SO-101 assembly guide (written "Fall 2026") says "Connect cables to both ports on the J5 motor before attaching this assembly to the arm", "Route the cables through the printed part and leave slack for movement", and "After attachment, move the wrist by hand to check for binding or cable tension." — [StevenZhoooooooooooooou/so101-assembly-guide](https://github.com/StevenZhoooooooooooooou/so101-assembly-guide)
- **LeRobot assumes the joint is continuous:** Stock `SOFollower.calibrate` / `SOLeader.calibrate` leave `wrist_roll` out of `record_ranges_of_motion` and assign it `0-4095` directly, which the PR author says rests on "the assumption that the joint turns freely". — [PR #4583](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/4583); also described in [issue #3193](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/3193) (comment by Dicomsky, 2026-03-21)
- **Gear ratios differ, ranges do not:** The follower uses STS3215 at 1/345 on all joints. The leader wrist roll (motor 5) uses 1/147. — [Hugging Face LeRobot SO-101 docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/so101). Waveshare's kit uses "ST3215 Servo (SE)" units on the leader; the product page does not explain "(SE)". — [Waveshare SO-ARM100/101 wiki](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/SO-ARM100/101)

### Inferences
- **Expected range:** about 340° (roughly 3850-3920 of 4096 counts) stop-to-stop on a correct build. User's follower: ~154° (~45% of normal). User's leader: ~255° (~75% of normal). Both are short. The follower is very short.
- **What limits the follower:** With torque off, an STS3215 output turns freely, so the ~154° limit cannot come from inside the servo or from which spline tooth the horn sits on. Moving the horn by a spline tooth only moves where the travel sits on the 0-4095 encoder scale. It does not change the travel's width. The limit must be one of:
  1. The servo 5 to servo 6 cable being too short, routed through the wrong F8 port, wrapped the wrong way, or pinched between F8 and F9.
  2. F9 contacting F8 somewhere other than the designed stop (resin flash or support nubs, a warped or out-of-tolerance resin part, or a screw head proud of a surface).
  3. Both together.
- **How the cable and the stop interact (inference):** The usable range is the overlap of the arc the cable allows and the ~340° arc the printed stop allows. If F9 was screwed to the horn at an orientation where the cable is already near its slack limit at the stop, the overlap can shrink a lot. That could plausibly produce ~154° with nothing broken.
- **The leader's 255° is also below the 338°-345° reference.** This may be hand-measurement error or a similar but milder cable or debris problem. The Waveshare SE leader may also differ from the TheRobotStudio leader geometry (unverified).
- **Why this overloads (inference, consistent with sources):** LeRobot maps wrist_roll to a full 0-4095 span on both arms without recording the real travel. When the leader moves through its ~255°, the follower is commanded to positions past its ~154° physical limit. At 1/345 gearing and up to 30 kg.cm, the follower servo pushes its printed parts or cable against the stop until its protection trips (flashing LED). The earlier symptom (follower turned only ~25% as far as the leader) fits the same short mechanical range.

### Gaps
- No source gives an official TheRobotStudio or Waveshare wrist roll range figure. The 338°-345° numbers come from four community-measured arms in one LeRobot PR.
- No source confirms whether the Waveshare resin F8/F9 are geometrically identical to TheRobotStudio's `Wrist_Roll_Pitch_SO101` / `Wrist_Roll_Follower_SO101`, or whether the resin kit's stop sits in the same place.
- No source gives a required slack length for the servo 5 to servo 6 cable, or says how much roll the stock-length cable allows.
- No source measures range for a Waveshare "ST3215 Servo (SE)" leader specifically.

## Q2. Which assembly mistakes cause a wrist roll stall or overload (horn orientation, cable wrap or bind, servo 5 upside down, F9 rubbing on F8, long screws)?

### Takeaway
The documented causes are printed-part contact at the wrist and cables that are too tight, pinched or snagged. Waveshare warns that servo 5's wire is easy to tear on insertion, and builders say to leave slack and hand-check for binding. Horn spline orientation is documented as mattering for calibration and zero alignment, not for passive range. No source documents servo 5 inserted upside down or over-long screws as a cause; those remain plausible but unverified.

### Cited Findings
- **Printed-part contact is the documented stop mechanism:** On a correctly built follower, the joint ends where "a corner of the printed gripper housing" meets "the wrist bracket" (PR #4583). That is the designed stop at about 340°. — [PR #4583](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/4583); designed protrusion stop confirmed in [TheRobotStudio issue #170](https://github.com/TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100/issues/170)
- **Waveshare's own warnings for servo 5:** Servo 5's orientation is "The side of the servo with the gear should be inserted first, with the gear facing up." The guide warns "Be careful when inserting the No. 5 servo, otherwise it is easy to tear the wire," and "If the servo is installed here before wiring, it will be difficult to install due to the limited space." For F8 in step 13: "Align F8 and F7 horizontally, with the rectangular opening of the wrist bracket F8 on the same side as the wiring port of the 4th servo." — [Waveshare assembly wiki](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/SO-ARM100/101_Kit_Aassembly)
- **One horn only on servo 5:** Waveshare: "Mount the geared steering wheel on one side, and do not install a steering wheel on the other side of the servo." HF docs: "Install only one motor horn on the wrist motor and secure it with a M3x6mm horn screw." The StevenZhou guide: "J5 uses only this one horn; do not fit a horn on the opposite side," and "We strongly recommend removing the horn from the splined side of motor F5 or L5" before inserting the motor. — [Waveshare wiki](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/SO-ARM100/101_Kit_Aassembly); [HF SO-101 docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/so101); [so101-assembly-guide](https://github.com/StevenZhoooooooooooooou/so101-assembly-guide)
- **Cable binding checks recommended by builders:** "After attachment, move the wrist by hand to check for binding or cable tension." For the final check: "Slowly move every unpowered joint through its range and look for pulled leads, pinched cables, or loose plugs." For a cable that unplugs during movement: "Turn off power, reroute it with slack through the full J2-J5 range, then secure it in clips." When fitting J4 (wrist flex), "leave a path for the J5/J6 cables". — [so101-assembly-guide](https://github.com/StevenZhoooooooooooooou/so101-assembly-guide)
- **Fit cables before seating motors:** "Insert one motor cable into each motor before mounting it in place... Once the arm is fully assembled, connecting the cables can become very difficult due to limited space." — [Artur Habuda build tutorial (2025-07-01)](https://arturhabuda.com/2025/07/01/build-tutorial-so-101-robot-arms/). HF docs: "It is advisable to install one 3-pin cable in the motor after placing them before continuing assembly", and use "the wire guides everywhere". — [HF SO-101 docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/so101); [HF assemble_so101 (main)](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/main/en/assemble_so101)
- **Support material:** "Remove all support material from the 3D-printed parts. The easiest way to do this is using a small screwdriver to get underneath the support material." — [HF SO-101 docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/so101)
- **Specified screw lengths:** Motor 5 to wrist holder: 2× M2x6mm "front screws". Horn: M3x6mm horn screw. Wrist to motor 4: 4× M3x6mm per side. Gripper (F9) to servo 5's horn: 4× M3x6mm. — [HF SO-101 docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/so101)
- **Blinking LED = stall at a limit:** A community SO-101 control panel README says a blinking servo LED usually means the servo is stalled, and moving it away from the mechanical limit typically clears it. This was seen only in a search-result summary. The page was not opened, so this is unverified. — [bkbk-robot/so101-arm](https://github.com/bkbk-robot/so101-arm)
- **Horn and zero alignment affects teleop mapping, not range:** In issue #3193, users report leader and follower wrist zeros about 180° apart, causing a jump at the half-turn. The maintainer said "The position of the wrist motor you start the calibration with will be the '0'" and that the mismatch goes away if both grippers are aligned before calibrating. — [lerobot issue #3193](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/3193)
- **Software-side contributor (adjacent):** A SO-100 report describes wrist_roll moving unexpectedly in teleop. The reporter suspected the default P coefficient was too high and that motion of other joints (elbow_flex) was correlated with it. This was seen only in a search summary; it is an SO-100, not SO-101, case and is not verified here. — [lerobot issue #1333](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/1333)

### Inferences
**Ranked likely causes for the user's case (154° follower, torque off):**
1. **Servo 5 to servo 6 cable too tight, wrong port, or pinched.** It is the only part that crosses the roll joint besides F9 itself. Waveshare stresses limited space and wire tearing at this exact step. Check: undo F9 from servo 5's horn, or unplug the 5 to 6 lead, and re-measure the roll by hand. If the range jumps to ~340° with the cable free, the cable is the limit. Fix: re-route it through the port Waveshare names, leave a loop of slack, and center that slack at the middle of the stop-to-stop travel. Also inspect the cable for nicks; Waveshare warns it tears easily.
2. **F9 touching F8 early.** Possible sources are resin support nubs or flash, a warped resin part, a cable lump trapped between the parts, or a screw head that stands proud. Check: with the cable unplugged, rotate slowly and see or feel where contact happens. If the contact is not the designed protrusion, trim or sand the high spot, or reprint or replace the part. With M2x6 and M3x6 specified, a longer screw substituted from the kit bag could protrude into the rotating gap (inference; no source documents this).
3. **Servo 5 seated upside down, or the horn on the wrong side.** Waveshare requires "gear facing up" with the horn on the splined side only. A reversed servo would put the output on the other face and could misplace F9 relative to F8's stop. Unverified: no source documents this failure. It is easy to spot visually.
4. **Horn spline orientation alone.** This cannot shorten passive range, because the servo has no mechanical limit. It does decide where the encoder 0/4095 seam falls and how the leader and follower zeros line up, so it matters for calibration and overload during teleop (see Q3).

**Why the overload follows:** In teleop, any range mismatch (follower 154° vs leader 255°, or a leader/follower zero offset) means the follower is commanded into its stop. The 1/345 high-torque follower then stalls against plastic and the cable, and its overload protection trips (flashing LED). Fixing the mechanical range first, then recalibrating, addresses the root cause. Continuing to teleoperate as-is risks tearing the 5 to 6 cable or cracking resin parts. That risk is inferred from the 30 kg.cm stall torque and Waveshare's tear warning.

### Gaps
- I found no GitHub issue, forum or blog post that specifically documents "wrist roll only ~150°" or "gripper cable limits wrist roll" on an SO-101. The cable-limit diagnosis is inferred from the Waveshare routing steps and builder slack advice.
- I found no source on resin-specific fit problems (shrinkage, brittleness) for Waveshare's resin SO-ARM101 parts.
- I found no source documenting servo 5 inserted upside down, or too-long screws, as a cause of wrist roll binding.
- I found no TheRobotStudio or Waveshare troubleshooting page for servo overload or a flashing LED. The Waveshare SO-ARM100/101 main wiki has none. The Seeed wiki FAQ covers an LED that is off (loose upstream cable) but not a flashing one. — [Waveshare SO-ARM100/101 wiki](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/SO-ARM100/101); [Seeed SO-Arm LeRobot tutorial](https://wiki.seeedstudio.com/lerobot_so100m_new/)

## Q3. How do builders center servo 5 before attaching the horn and wrist (middle position 2048)?

### Takeaway
Neither the official HF docs nor the Waveshare guide includes a pre-assembly centering step. The community practice is to put the servo at raw 2048 (or write its current position as the 2048 mid-point with a vendor tool), then attach F9 with the gripper in a neutral, untwisted pose. The middle of the physical stop-to-stop travel should then sit near 2048, so the encoder seam falls in the blocked ~20° sector. Calibrate afterwards with leader and follower wrists in the same pose.

### Cited Findings
- **No official centering step:** The Waveshare assembly page contains no instruction to center the servo before fitting the horn, and no wrist roll range notes (checked in full). HF docs say only that, at calibration, "you need to move the robot to the position where all joints are in the middle of their ranges", then sweep each joint. — [Waveshare assembly wiki](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/SO-ARM100/101_Kit_Aassembly); [HF SO-101 docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/so101)
- **Community centering guide:** A third-party setup guide says to center all motors to 2048: "Do this BEFORE assembling the arm (or loosen joints if already assembled)", because "the motors at random positions triggers the 'Magnitude exceeds 2047' error". Then "assemble (or adjust) the arm so it is in the NEUTRAL POSE", with Wrist Roll (5) at "no twist" and the gripper half open. The guide includes a Python script based on the scservo SDK. Undated; third-party. — [Charan-mdnl/so-arm101-lerobot-setup guide](https://github.com/Charan-mdnl/so-arm101-lerobot-setup/blob/main/so_arm101_setup_guide.md)
- **Vendor mid-point tool (Seeed):** Seeed's SoARM tool `servo_middle_calibration.py` writes the current position as the middle value 2048, and `servo_center_test.py` checks that the servo returns to 2048. Then: "After the middle-position calibration, return to the lerobot-calibrate steps above and redo the full-arm calibration." Seeed's FAQ links "Magnitude ... exceeds 2047" to an abnormal zero offset or position, and suggests restarting and recalibrating, then the middle-position tool. — [Seeed SO-Arm LeRobot tutorial](https://wiki.seeedstudio.com/lerobot_so100m_new/)
- **Builder fix using mid-point calibration:** In issue #3193, twdent (2026-08-25) fixed a leader/follower wrist_roll zero mismatch "using middle calibration in Seeed's RoboController tool", which showed the servo's middle was not at 2048. — [lerobot issue #3193](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/3193)
- **Workaround in LeRobot 0.5.1:** Before calibrating, rotate wrist_roll by hand until its raw Present_Position reads about 2048, using a short FeetechMotorsBus read loop. Otherwise `set_half_turn_homings()` raises "Magnitude 2073 exceeds 2047". The issue was closed 2026-04-14, "fixed in #3373". — [lerobot issue #3193](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/3193)
- **Why the seam placement matters:** `set_half_turn_homings` centers the real travel on 2048. On the measured follower, "(113 + 3981) / 2 = 2047", so the ~11° the joint cannot reach lands on the encoder seam. The PR's second commit zeroes wrist_roll at the middle of its swept travel rather than at the ENTER pose. — [PR #4583](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/4583)
- **Horn screws last:** One search summary said a build guide fits horn screws last so the output can still be centered. The specific source page was not identified or opened, so this is unverified. (Search result set included [makerguides SO-ARM101 Win11 guide](https://www.makerguides.com/getting-started-so-arm101-win11/) and [Hiwonder SO-ARM101 manual](https://docs.hiwonder.com/projects/LeRobot/en/latest/docs/SO_ARM101_Open_Source_6_Axis_Robotic_Arm_User_Manual.html).)

### Inferences
**A practical re-assembly order for the user's follower wrist (inferred from the sources above, not an official procedure):**
1. Power off. Remove F10 if it is in the way, then remove the 4 M3x6 screws holding F9 to servo 5's horn.
2. Re-route the 5 to 6 cable with a slack loop, and confirm servo 5 is seated "gear facing up".
3. With F9 detached, put servo 5 at raw ~2048. Either command it there, or use the Waveshare/Feetech or Seeed tool to write the mid-point at a chosen pose.
4. Re-fit F9 so the gripper is in its neutral, untwisted orientation and the designed stop protrusion is roughly opposite neutral, with equal travel each way.
5. Hand-check about ±170° of travel with no cable tension.
6. Then run `lerobot-calibrate` on both arms, starting from the same physical wrist pose.

Steps 3 and 6 write servo EEPROM or talk to hardware, so the user must do them. If the horn is screwed to F9 at an arbitrary orientation, the blocked sector may sit away from the encoder seam. With LeRobot's synthetic 0-4095 wrist_roll range, that can make the follower try to cross its stop, which causes an overload even after the cable is fixed.

### Gaps
- No official TheRobotStudio, HF or Waveshare document prescribes centering servo 5 at 2048 before fitting its horn. This is community practice only.
- The Waveshare "Set Servo ID" and calibration sub-pages were not fetched. They may describe a mid-point or "middle position" function in Waveshare's own servo tool. Not verified.

## Q4. Official TheRobotStudio SO-ARM100 notes, errata or printed-part revisions about the wrist or wrist roll cable routing

### Takeaway
TheRobotStudio's README lists no known issues or errata and no revision notes for the wrist parts. The only official wrist-relevant facts are that the SO-101 has separate leader and follower wrist roll parts, both with a calibration stop, and that the SO-101 has "improved wiring". Recent repo activity on the wrist is camera mounts and URDFs, not wrist roll mechanics.

### Cited Findings
- **README:** The SO-101 "has improved wiring, is easier to assemble (no gear removal) and uses updated motors for the leader arm". The README has no known-issues or errata section and gives no revision notes for `Wrist_Roll_SO101.stl` (leader), `Wrist_Roll_Follower_SO101.stl` (follower) or `Wrist_Roll_Pitch_SO101.stl` (common). It does not mention calibration stops, horn alignment or cable routing, and points to the HF assembly guide. It recommends PLA+, 15% infill, no supports in horizontal screw holes, and checking fit with the servo gauges ("Gauge Zero and Gauge Tight"). — [TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100 README](https://github.com/TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100)
- **Issue #170 (2026-07-23 to 2026-08-15):** Confirms the SO-101 wrist roll parts (both leader and follower) include a calibration stop. The SO-100 wrist did not. — [issue #170](https://github.com/TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100/issues/170)
- **Issue #169 (opened 2026-07-13, open):** A docs typo said the leader handle attaches "to motor 5". It was corrected in HF docs v0.6.1 to "Attach the handle to the leader holder using 1 M2x6mm screw". dmosher42 notes the written steps don't match the video order. — [issue #169](https://github.com/TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100/issues/169)
- **Other wrist-tagged repo items (title-level only):** #47 "Duplicate part Wrist_Roll_Pitch_08i-1 on Print_Leader_Bambu_P1P.3mf" (2025-03-18). Several wrist camera mount PRs and issues (#50, #85, #110, #114, #159, #166). URDF/MuJoCo PRs #176 and #186 (Sep 2026). None concerns wrist roll travel or cable routing. — [TheRobotStudio issues search "wrist"](https://github.com/TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100/issues?q=wrist)
- **HF docs inconsistency:** The HF `main` assemble page's leader gear table labels "Wrist Roll → Motor 4" and "Wrist Pitch → Motor 5", while the config maps `"wrist_roll": [5, "sts3215"]`. The current SO-101 page lists Wrist Roll = motor 5 (1/147). The user has already verified ID 5 = wrist roll on both arms. — [HF assemble_so101 (main)](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/main/en/assemble_so101); [HF SO-101 docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/so101)
- **Software-side change in progress:** LeRobot PR #4583 (open as of Oct 2026) would sweep and record wrist_roll's real range instead of assuming 0-4095, and zero it at mid-travel. This would make LeRobot respect each arm's actual stops. — [PR #4583](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/4583)

### Inferences
- Since no official errata exist, the user's fix has to be mechanical (cable slack and routing, clearing interference, correct seating), then recalibration. Upstream LeRobot still treats wrist_roll as a full turn, so a short follower range will keep causing overloads in teleop until the mechanics are fixed or the range is recorded (PR #4583 behaviour).

### Gaps
- Page 2 of TheRobotStudio's "wrist" issue search (2 results) and issue bodies beyond titles were not read.
- Waveshare publishes no errata or revision history for F8/F9 that I could find.
- The LeRobot Discord was not searched (not web-accessible).
