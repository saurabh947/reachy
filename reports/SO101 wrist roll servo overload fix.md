# Unblock the follower wrist, then recalibrate both

> **Update 2026-10-13: measured, and the mechanical diagnosis below is wrong.** The ~154° and ~255° figures came from a quick twist test, not a stop-to-stop measurement. A read-only reading at each stop shows normal travel on both arms:
>
> | Arm | End 1 | End 2 | Travel |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Follower | raw 284 (−155.0°) | raw 4048 (+175.9°) | ≈331° |
> | Leader | raw 158 (−166.1°) | raw 3908 (+163.6°) | ≈330° |
>
> Nothing is blocking the wrist.
>
> **Later the same day, logged teleop showed no overload problem with the current calibration.** Two full-torque runs, idle for 23 s and then 131 s with the leader swept to both ends, logged every frame at about 52 Hz:
> - The goal sent to servo 5 always equalled the leader's angle.
> - With the leader still, the follower tracked within 0.27° (median).
> - The status flag stayed clear and the load never passed 80%. It peaked at 64%, briefly, during fast moves.
> - With the leader at its left end (−166.4°), the powered follower stopped at −160.8°, not the −155° measured by hand. That left a 5.6° gap at about 27% load, far below the overload trip of more than 80% for 2 s.
>
> So the proposed offset and position-limit writes are **not needed** and were not applied. Servo 5 also passed direct step tests at 25% and full torque. The first overload is explained by the earlier calibration, which was 124° off. A second overload after recalibration could not be reproduced and remains unexplained.

Apply the mechanical fix first, because something is physically blocking the follower's wrist roll. Every SO-101 wrist measured in public travels **338–345° stop to stop** against a designed printed stop. The ST3215/STS3215 has no internal end stop, so a follower that turns only **~154° by hand with power off** has something extra in the way. The most likely culprits are the servo 5→6 cable that crosses the roll joint, or the gripper housing (F9) hitting the wrist bracket (F8) early. LeRobot 0.6.1 hides this defect. It treats wrist_roll as a free-spinning 0–4095 joint and writes 0/4095 "limits" that limit nothing. It also copies the leader's degrees to the follower without clamping. The frictionless SE leader (~255°) can therefore command angles the follower cannot reach. The follower pushes into its stop. Once its load stays above 80% for 2 s, Feetech protection trips and the LED flashes. Every later status reply then carries the overload bit, which is why the disconnect's `Torque_Enable` write fails with "[RxPacketError] Overload error!". Your matched-pose recalibration could not fix this. Matching the poses lines up the two zeros, but it cannot shrink a 255° leader sweep to fit a 154° follower. No public report matches this exact symptom on a Waveshare SE kit. The diagnosis is pieced together from LeRobot code, Feetech datasheets and four measured arms, not from one confirmed case. The order is: power down and check by hand (no writes); restore ~340° of travel; recalibrate both arms together from the same centred pose (writes); and only then add software range limits or torque caps (writes) as a backstop. Stop running teleop until then: a LeRobot maintainer has diagnosed a permanently broken SO-101 servo caused by sustained overload ([lerobot #2819](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/2819#issuecomment-3768335194)).

**Convention used below:** every step that writes to a servo register, commands motion, or runs a LeRobot command that does either is tagged **[SERVO WRITE]**. Each one needs your explicit approval before it runs. Untagged steps are hands-on or strictly read-only.

## A 154° wrist on a 340° design means something is in the way

A correctly built SO-101 wrist roll does not spin freely. It has a designed printed hard stop. In LeRobot PR #4583, one follower is blocked at raw counts 113 and 3981, about **340°**. The author describes the stop as "two printed parts touching", a corner of the gripper housing meeting the wrist bracket, not a gear stop. A reviewer measured two more arms at **342.9° (follower) and 338.0° (leader)**. That makes "four arms with a stop, between 338° and 345°, and none that spins" ([lerobot PR #4583](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/4583)). TheRobotStudio contributors confirm that both the SO-101 leader and follower wrist parts include this calibration stop ([TheRobotStudio #170](https://github.com/TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100/issues/170)). The MuJoCo Menagerie model was corrected to about 320° of wrist roll ([mujoco_menagerie PR #324](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco_menagerie/pull/324)). Across the measured pairs, neither the leader nor the follower is consistently the larger one, so the leader is not larger by design.

The servo cannot be what limits travel. Waveshare lists the ST3215's mechanical limit angle as "No Limit" ([Waveshare ST3215](https://www.waveshare.com/product/robotics/motors-servos/servos/st3215-servo.htm)), and the servo powers up with torque disabled ([AIFitLab STS table](https://aifitlab-wiki.super.site/feetech-servo-motor-docs/feetech-sts-servo-memory-table-analysis)). A follower that stops at ~154° with no power must therefore be blocked by something outside the servo. Which spline tooth the horn sits on cannot cause it either: that only moves where the travel sits on the encoder scale, not how wide it is. One part besides F9 itself crosses the roll joint: the servo 5→6 cable. Waveshare's guide routes it out of the bottom of the fixed wrist bracket F8 and into servo 6, which rides in F9 on servo 5's horn. The guide also warns that "it is easy to tear the wire" when inserting servo 5 into its tight space ([Waveshare assembly wiki](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/SO-ARM100/101_Kit_Aassembly)). Builder guides tell you to "leave slack for movement" at J5 and, after fitting it, to "move the wrist by hand to check for binding or cable tension" ([so101-assembly-guide](https://github.com/StevenZhoooooooooooooou/so101-assembly-guide)). The likely causes, in order, are: the cable is too short, in the wrong port, wrapped the wrong way, or pinched between F8 and F9; F9 touches F8 before the designed stop because of resin flash, support nubs, a warped part or a proud screw head; or servo 5 is seated wrong. Waveshare specifies "gear facing up" with a single horn on the splined side ([Waveshare assembly wiki](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/SO-ARM100/101_Kit_Aassembly); [HF SO-101 docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/so101)).

Your leader's ~255° is also well below the reference. That suggests a milder version of the same problem, hand-measurement error, or SE-leader geometry that no source documents. Two caveats: no source reports a ~154° follower, and none confirms that Waveshare's resin F8/F9 parts match TheRobotStudio's geometry. If the CAM kit puts the camera or its lead near the wrist, include it in the inspection; this is a hypothesis, not a documented cause.

## LeRobot 0.6.1 turns that obstruction into a two-second stall

SO-101 recorded wrist_roll's range like any other joint up to v0.4.2. **PR #2780 (shipped in v0.4.3)** merged the SO-100 and SO-101 classes and inherited SO-100's `full_turn_motor = "wrist_roll"` ([PR #2780](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/2780)). In v0.6.1, `calibrate()` sets the ENTER pose to read about 2047 and skips the range sweep for wrist_roll. It sets that joint's range to 0–4095 on both arms ([so_follower.py v0.6.1](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/v0.6.1/src/lerobot/robots/so_follower/so_follower.py#L115-L157)), and `write_calibration` writes those values as `Min/Max_Position_Limit`, which in effect means **no limit** ([feetech.py v0.6.1](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/v0.6.1/src/lerobot/motors/feetech/feetech.py#L268-L276)). Maintainers closed proposals to restore range recording, saying wrist roll is expected to be full-turn ([PR #3196](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/3196)). With `use_degrees=True`, the default on both arms, the leader's angle passes to the follower 1:1. The DEGREES path in `_unnormalize` does **not** clamp to the calibrated range ([motors_bus.py v0.6.1](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/v0.6.1/src/lerobot/motors/motors_bus.py#L854-L911)), and a maintainer confirmed this in review ([PR #3615](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/3615)).

The arithmetic is unforgiving. If both arms were calibrated with the wrist centred, the follower reaches its stop about **±77°** from zero, while the leader can roll about **±127°**. Roughly 50° at each end of the leader's sweep is unreachable for the follower. If the follower's ENTER pose sat off-centre in its short travel, one stop is even closer. Teleop writes the leader's pose as `Goal_Position` on the very first frame ([so_follower.py v0.6.1](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/v0.6.1/src/lerobot/robots/so_follower/so_follower.py#L204-L230)). A `Goal_Position` write re-energises an STS3215 even when torque was off ([feetech-cli](https://pypi.org/project/feetech-cli/0.1.0/); [lerobot #3585](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/3585)). A goal even slightly past the stop leaves a position error the servo can never close, so its output stays high. The SE leader gives the operator no warning: Waveshare calls it "a virtual servo, which only has an encoder and no power section", so nothing resists the hand ([Waveshare assembly wiki](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/SO-ARM100/101_Kit_Aassembly)). LeRobot also sets `Acceleration = 254`, the maximum, so these moves are abrupt ([feetech.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/lerobot/main/src/lerobot/motors/feetech/feetech.py)). The closest public case is twdent's Seeed SO-101 with wrist hard stops in #3193: the follower "jumped when rolling past halfway and hit a hard stop" ([lerobot #3193](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/3193#issuecomment-5386125382)).

Feetech's official 12 V ST3215 datasheet explains the rest. A servo stalled above **80% of stall torque for 2 s** enters overload protection, and re-sending a position command clears the flag ([Feetech ST-3215-C047 datasheet](https://files.seeedstudio.com/products/Feetech/108090003_FEETECH_ST-3215-C047-Datasheet.pdf)). Your servo 5 registers match the factory defaults: `Overload_Torque` 80, `Protection_Time` 200 (×10 ms = 2.0 s), and `Protective_Torque` 20, so the servo drops to 20% output instead of going limp ([PR #4783](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/4783); [AIFitLab STS table](https://aifitlab-wiki.super.site/feetech-servo-motor-docs/feetech-sts-servo-memory-table-analysis)). While the flag is latched, every reply packet carries ERROR bit 5 (0x20) ([Feetech protocol manual](https://files.seeedstudio.com/wiki/robotics/Actuator/feetech/Communication_Protocol_Manual.pdf)). LeRobot turns any non-zero error byte into a `RuntimeError`, even when the packet arrived intact ([motors_bus.py v0.6.1](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/v0.6.1/src/lerobot/motors/motors_bus.py#L1085-L1125)). So the "failed" `Torque_Enable` write is the latched overload being reported, not necessarily a rejected write. The "after 6 tries" in the message is misleading, because status-bit errors are never retried. The disconnect path makes things worse. v0.6.1 disables torque motor by motor, **stops at the first exception**, and skips `closePort()` ([motors_bus.py v0.6.1](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/v0.6.1/src/lerobot/motors/motors_bus.py#L547-L562)). The gripper (ID 6) is left energised and the port stays open. Open PR #4695 fixes this ([PR #4695](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/4695)). LeRobot caps torque and current only on the gripper (`Max_Torque_Limit` 500, `Protection_Current` 250, `Overload_Torque` 25). The wrist runs at full factory torque ([so_follower.py v0.6.1](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/v0.6.1/src/lerobot/robots/so_follower/so_follower.py#L159-L171)). That makes repeated stalls a real risk to the servo and to the resin parts.

## Hands-on and read-only checks settle the diagnosis

One trap first: several LeRobot entry points that look harmless are not read-only. Connecting the robot runs `configure()`, which writes acceleration, PID, the Phase bit and gripper protection values inside a `torque_disabled()` block that **re-enables torque on exit** ([so_follower.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/lerobot/main/src/lerobot/robots/so_follower/so_follower.py)). `lerobot-calibrate` writes `Homing_Offset` and position limits. `lerobot-teleoperate` writes goals every frame. Any bus diagnostic should be a minimal script that only issues reads, like the short `FeetechMotorsBus` read loop the #3193 reporter used ([lerobot #3193](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/3193)).

| # | Check (no servo writes) | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | (Optional, before step 2) Read register `Status` (65) on ID 5 with a reads-only script, with LeRobot's error raising off | Bit 5 set confirms overload. Bit 0 = voltage, bit 3 = over-current, which point elsewhere ([AIFitLab](https://aifitlab-wiki.super.site/feetech-servo-motor-docs/feetech-sts-servo-memory-table-analysis)) |
| 2 | Unplug the 12 V follower supply **and** USB for ~5 s, then reconnect power only if needed | Clears the latched flag and leaves the servo limp. USB alone can keep a small current flowing ([Classmethod](https://dev.classmethod.jp/en/articles/lerobot-so-arm101-assembly-teleop/); [rigbetellabs](https://github.com/rigbetellabs/S0101)) |
| 3 | With power off, turn the follower wrist by hand and feel for stiffness or grit | Stiffness after a power cycle points to a damaged servo ([#2819](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/2819); [#2693](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/2693)) |
| 4 | Roll the follower stop to stop slowly and watch what touches at each end: a taut cable, F9's corner on F8, a screw head, a resin nub | Shows the blocking feature directly |
| 5 | Unplug the 5→6 cable at servo 6 (or remove F9's four M3×6 horn screws) and re-measure | If travel jumps toward ~340°, the cable is the limit |
| 6 | Inspect servo 5's seating (gear up, one horn on the splined side), screw lengths (M2×6, M3×6), and the cable for nicks | Rules out assembly errors ([HF SO-101 docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/so101)) |
| 7 | Repeat step 4 on the leader (~255°) | Tells you whether the leader has a similar, milder obstruction |
| 8 | Open both calibration JSONs in `~/.cache/huggingface/lerobot/calibration/` and confirm wrist_roll is 0/4095 on both, with `use_degrees` the same on both configs | Rules out a stale pre-v0.4.3 file or a unit mismatch like [#3176](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/3176) |
| 9 | With torque off, hold the follower at each stop and read `Present_Position` with a reads-only script | If the midpoint of the two readings is far from ~2047, the calibration pose was off-centre in the follower's short travel ([PR #4583](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/4583)) |
| 10 | Check the follower supply label is 12 V and at least 5 A | A 12 V 2 A supply has browned out SO-101 arms ([andymai/so-arm101](https://github.com/andymai/so-arm101)). This rules out a power cause; it does not explain an overload |

Steps 4 and 5 are decisive. If the follower reaches ~340° once the cable is freed or F9 is unbolted, the root cause is confirmed and no register change is needed to fix it. If travel stays short with F9 removed, contact is happening inside the F8/servo 5 assembly, which points to seating or a deformed part.

## Ranked fixes, with every servo write flagged

| Rank | Fix | Evidence | Servo writes? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Restore ~340° of mechanical travel: re-route the 5→6 cable with a slack loop centred at mid-travel, remove interference, re-seat servo 5 | Builder guidance plus the stop geometry in PR #4583; not confirmed for this exact symptom | **None** (hands-on) |
| 2 | Re-fit F9 so the neutral gripper pose sits at mid-travel | Community practice | **None** if guided by reads; **[SERVO WRITE]** if using a "set middle = 2048" tool |
| 3 | Delete old calibration files, then recalibrate **both** arms together from an identical pose centred in the follower's travel | Maintainer advice; PR #4583 review | **[SERVO WRITE]** |
| 4 | Enforce the real range in software: host clamp, PR #4583/#4826 locally, or hand-set limits | Unmerged PRs; firmware clamping reported in #3585 | Clamp: code only. PRs and limits: **[SERVO WRITE]** |
| 5 | Seeed middle-position reset (write current position as 2048) | Confirmed fix for a 180° wrist desync | **[SERVO WRITE]** (EEPROM) |
| 6 | Damage limiters: per-joint `max_relative_target`, lower wrist torque or overload thresholds | Untested for this case | Config: none. Registers: **[SERVO WRITE]** |
| 7 | Recovery and clean disconnect | Repo practice | Power cycle: none. `Goal = Present`: **[SERVO WRITE]** |

**Rank 1 is the fix.** Re-route the 5→6 lead through the port Waveshare names. Leave a loop of slack that is relaxed when the wrist is at mid-travel. Trim or sand any resin high spot where F9 meets F8 outside the designed protrusion. Replace a torn or nicked cable, since Waveshare warns this wire tears easily ([Waveshare assembly wiki](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/SO-ARM100/101_Kit_Aassembly)). The target is the ~340° community norm. Anything near that moves your arm from "unusual" to "standard", and the rest of the LeRobot ecosystem is tuned for standard arms.

**Rank 2** puts the remaining ~20° blocked sector on the encoder seam, where it belongs. On a correct arm the midpoint of the two stops lands at about 2047, so the joint stays off the 0/4095 seam ([PR #4583](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/4583)). The read-only way: with the servo unpowered or torque off, read `Present_Position` while you choose the spline tooth for F9. The faster community way is to centre servo 5 at 2048 before fitting the horn, using Seeed's `servo_middle_calibration.py` ([Seeed tutorial](https://wiki.seeedstudio.com/lerobot_so100m_new/)), a scservo script ([Charan-mdnl guide](https://github.com/Charan-mdnl/so-arm101-lerobot-setup/blob/main/so_arm101_setup_guide.md)), or the datasheet's "write 128 to address 40" one-key centring ([Feetech datasheet](https://files.seeedstudio.com/products/Feetech/108090003_FEETECH_ST-3215-C047-Datasheet.pdf)). Each of these is a **[SERVO WRITE]**.

**Rank 3** is required after any mechanical change. Waveshare and Seeed both say to delete the old files in `.../calibration/robots` and `.../teleoperators` first, because LeRobot may otherwise reuse outdated offsets ([Waveshare calibration wiki](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/SO-ARM100/101_Robotic_Arm_Calibration_and_Remote_Control); [Seeed tutorial](https://wiki.seeedstudio.com/lerobot_so100m_new/)). Then run `lerobot-calibrate` on follower and leader in the same session **[SERVO WRITE]**, with both wrists in the same physical orientation at ENTER. The maintainer's position is that "the position of the wrist motor you start the calibration with will be the '0'" ([lerobot #3193](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/3193#issuecomment-5388080872)). PR #4583's author found that leader and follower stay aligned only if ENTER is within about **±10° of mid-travel** ([PR #4583 follow-up](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/4583#issuecomment-5825878184)). A reviewer warns that recalibrating only one arm of a pair leaves the zeros misaligned ([PR #4583](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/4583)).

**Rank 4 is a backstop, not a substitute.** The least invasive option is a local clamp of the wrist_roll goal in DEGREES mode, which a maintainer floated as a follow-up ([PR #3615](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/3615)). It is a code change only, although every teleop run still writes goals. Applying PR #4583 locally sweeps wrist_roll stop to stop and writes the measured range as `Min/Max_Position_Limit` **[SERVO WRITE]**. PR #4826's `--auto` mode deliberately drives every joint into both stops at limited torque **[SERVO WRITE, with motion]** ([PR #4826](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/4826)). Writing `Min/Max_Position_Limit` on ID 5 by hand needs `Lock = 0` for the values to persist **[SERVO WRITE, EEPROM]** ([Waveshare ST3215 wiki](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/ST3215_Servo)). Treat that option with care for three reasons. No community report confirms it as an overload fix. The next `lerobot-calibrate` overwrites it with 0/4095. And on one new SO-101, calibrated limits made shoulder_lift and wrist_flex drive the wrong way into their stops until they overloaded ([#3585 comment](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/3585#issuecomment-5816932404)).

**Rank 5** fixes a different fault. twdent's ~180° leader/follower wrist desync was fixed with "middle calibration in Seeed's RoboController tool" followed by `lerobot-calibrate` ([lerobot #3193](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/3193#issuecomment-5408003117); [Seeed RoboController](https://github.com/Seeed-Projects/Seeed_RoboController)). It realigns zeros but does not widen a 154° travel. Use it **[SERVO WRITE, EEPROM]** only if the wrist still jumps near a half-turn, or calibration throws "Magnitude … exceeds 2047", after ranks 1–3.

**Rank 6 reduces harm without removing the cause.** `max_relative_target` caps how far each goal can lead the measured position ([so_follower.py v0.6.1](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/v0.6.1/src/lerobot/robots/so_follower/so_follower.py#L204-L230)). It is a config flag that writes no registers, but it is a velocity cap, not a range limit ([#1527](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/1527)). The wrist still reaches the stop, and a cap has been shown to add large lag ([hiwonder-SoArm-101 PR #3](https://github.com/yolo-jared/hiwonder-SoArm-101/pull/3)). Lowering `Max_Torque_Limit` (16), `Overload_Torque` (36) or `Protection_Time` (35) on ID 5 mirrors what LeRobot already does for the gripper and shortens each stall **[SERVO WRITE, EEPROM]**. Upstream stall detection and a startup-mismatch guard are still open PRs ([PR #4447](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/4447); [PR #3954](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/3954)).

**Rank 7 handles the aftermath.** Prefer a full power cycle (12 V and USB) to clear the latch. The official alternative is a new position command, ideally `Goal_Position = Present_Position` to avoid a jump. That is a **[SERVO WRITE]** that also re-energises the servo, and after power-up `Goal_Position` reads 0, so writing back a stale goal would swing the wrist a long way ([feetech-cli](https://pypi.org/project/feetech-cli/0.1.0/); [PR #4826](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/4826)). Passing `--robot.disable_torque_on_disconnect=False` skips the torque-off writes that currently raise ([config_so_follower.py v0.6.1](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/v0.6.1/src/lerobot/robots/so_follower/config_so_follower.py#L31-L47)). The arm then stays energised, so cut power right after. If the wrist is stiff with power off after all this, replace the servo, as the #2819 reporter did ([lerobot #2819](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/2819)).

## Four claims still lack a confirmed case

Four parts of the diagnosis are inferred. First, no LeRobot issue reports the exact `Torque_Enable` / ID 5 overload string, and no source pairs an SE leader with a wrist overload ([lerobot issues: Overload](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues?q=Overload)). Second, the community has not confirmed that firmware position limits on wrist_roll prevent the stall. Third, the auto-energise-on-goal behaviour was measured on firmware 3.10 and an unspecified unit, not on your 3.9 ([lerobot #3585](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/3585)). Fourth, Waveshare does not document whether the SE encoder reports 4096 counts per turn in the same direction as a geared ST3215. These gaps do not change the ranking: a 186° shortfall in passive travel, visible with power off, has to be fixed mechanically whichever software path you take next.

## Conclusion

Overload protection is doing its job here; the real fault is a geometry mismatch. Since v0.4.3, LeRobot's full-turn wrist_roll mapping has assumed every arm's wrist can follow wherever the leader goes. That holds only when both arms have near-identical ~340° travel and centred zeros. Your follower breaks the first condition by about 186°, and the SE leader's lack of resistance removes the one cue that would warn the operator. Matched-pose recalibration was the right move for zero alignment, but it could never fix a range mismatch. That explains why the overload survived it.

Fix the mechanics before writing anything. Position limits, middle-position resets or torque caps derived from a 154° wrist would bake the defect into EEPROM. LeRobot's next calibration would then overwrite some of them silently, and they would hide a cable that is being strained on every move. Once the wrist travels ~340°, a single recalibration of both arms **[SERVO WRITE]** should be enough for normal teleop. A host-side clamp is cheap insurance until PR #4583 or #4826 lands upstream.
